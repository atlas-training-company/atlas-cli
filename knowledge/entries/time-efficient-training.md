# Time-efficient training

Evidence: A superset review found shorter sessions with broadly comparable repetitions and volume load, but higher perceived exertion and metabolic demand. Only three of its 19 studies examined chronic adaptations, so confidence in equal long-term growth is limited. [zhang-2025-supersets]

Evidence: Single-set training produced gains over eight weeks in young trained adults even when sets stopped at two RIR. This supports a useful time-limited option, not proof that one set maximizes growth. [hermann-2025-without-fail]

Practice inference: On a compressed schedule, preserve a few purposeful working sets or pair non-competing accessories when setup is simple. Allow more recovery if repetitions or execution deteriorate. Dense pairings of demanding exercises can defeat the purpose by reducing useful work. Choose the tradeoff explicitly: reduced session time and maximal possible adaptation are different objectives. These are general adult training options rather than a complete replacement program.
