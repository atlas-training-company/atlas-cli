# Aerobic conditioning

Guideline: WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity, 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, or an equivalent combination each week for substantial adult health benefits, alongside muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days. Some activity benefits health below those totals; begin with manageable amounts and increase gradually. These are public-health recommendations, not a mandatory first-week workload or an optimal endurance-performance plan. [who-2020-physical-activity]

Intensity guidance: Relative intensity depends on the person. CDC's talk-test cue is being able to converse but finding singing difficult at moderate intensity; vigorous work permits only brief speech before another breath is needed. This is a practical estimate, not a laboratory threshold, a diagnosis, or a precise heart-rate zone. [cdc-2025-intensity]

Coaching procedure: Start from reported and logged activity, the training priority, available time, and a tolerable modality. Author duration and an intelligible effort target; use pace or heart rate only when the context supports interpreting them. Include existing walking, cycling, sport, or classes when relevant, without counting overlapping records twice. Record actual duration, intensity, and symptoms separately from the plan.

Coaching procedure: Increase a manageable component of the plan when recent sessions are repeatable and recovery is acceptable; review the response before adding more. Avoid assuming missing aerobic logs mean zero activity, prescribing hard intervals merely to fill a weekly quota, or treating every session labelled "Zone 2" as equivalent. When strength and aerobic work compete for time or recovery, consult the concurrent-training entry and preserve the person's stated priority.

Applicability: General adult health and introductory conditioning. Sport-specific performance targets, pregnancy, clinical rehabilitation, and other specialized populations need appropriate guidance beyond these general recommendations. An exact progression rate or interval prescription is not established here.
