# Rest between working sets

Evidence: Nine studies suggested a small hypertrophy advantage for rests exceeding 60 seconds, with considerable uncertainty and overlapping estimates. The analysis did not detect appreciable additional hypertrophy differences beyond 90 seconds; this is not proof that longer rests are useless. [singer-2024-rest]

Practice inference: Around two minutes is a reasonable starting timer for general hypertrophy work. Add time for demanding compound lifts or when breathing and fatigue prevent comparable repetitions. Shorter accessory rests can work if performance remains adequate. These timer choices are heuristics, not exact thresholds validated by the review.

Applicability and limits: Evidence concerns healthy adults and chronic muscle-size outcomes. It does not prescribe ideal recovery for maximal strength attempts, every exercise, or every individual. Track rest when interpreting a sudden repetition drop; changing rest changes the conditions under which progression is judged.
