# Adherence and recovery

Evidence: A review of 13 studies in insufficiently active adults found that specific goal-setting interventions improved physical activity. Studies lasted 3–24 weeks; this does not establish the best coaching method for experienced lifters, permanent adherence, or an optimal review interval. [garstang-2024-goal-setting]

Coaching procedure: Agree on a feasible behavior goal, a normal training opportunity, and a smaller fallback when time or equipment changes. Ask which obstacle caused a missed session rather than assume low motivation. Review completed, skipped, and unknown work separately. Missing data are a reason to clarify, not proof that the athlete failed the plan.

For progress reviews and authorized adjustments, retrieve `guides/review-adaptation`.

Sleep guidance: AASM/SRS recommends regularly obtaining at least seven hours of sleep for healthy adults aged 18–60, while acknowledging individual variation. Sleep quality and regularity also matter. This general-health consensus does not establish an athlete's exact sleep need or a rule to cancel training after one short night. [watson-2015-sleep]

Exercise timing: A review pooling 16 randomized trials found no clear morning advantage for most sleep outcomes. Evening/nighttime exercise was associated with more wakefulness after falling asleep, but possible small-study effects weaken confidence; differences appeared greater at higher intensities. The abstract does not establish a universal bedtime cutoff. [souabni-2026-exercise-timing-sleep]

Practice inference: Keep a feasible training window when sleep is satisfactory. If harder late sessions repeatedly disrupt sleep, try changing timing or intensity and review the response, rather than automatically abandoning evening exercise or sacrificing sleep to train early.

Nutrition and scope: Ask about sleep opportunity, stress, and food or fluid constraints when they help explain the training context. Keep nutritional suggestions general and responsive to the request; this entry supplies no calorie, protein, supplement, or hydration-dose prescription. Medical dietary needs, eating concerns, or persistent unexplained fatigue merit appropriate professional input. Do not diagnose under-fuelling or prescribe restriction from incomplete training logs.

Applicability: General adult coaching. Persistent symptoms or sleep concerns need individual assessment; a wearable score or one poor session alone does not establish overtraining or justify a lasting program change.
