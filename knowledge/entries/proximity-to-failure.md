# Proximity to failure and RIR

Evidence: Exploratory meta-regression associates closer-to-failure sets with greater hypertrophy, while strength gains were less dependent on estimated RIR. RIR was reconstructed from study descriptions, so this does not establish an exact optimal target. [robinson-2024-rir]

Evidence: In an eight-week single-set trial with 42 young trained adults, both failure and two-RIR groups improved. Some muscle-size outcomes modestly favored failure; strength and endurance gains were similar. This does not prove all sets must reach failure. [hermann-2025-without-fail]

Later trial: In 19 trained adults, an eight-week comparison assigned opposite legs to failure or RIR-based leg extensions. Estimated muscle-thickness and 1RM differences were small, with no clear advantage; the abstract describes 1–3 RIR as a viable option but does not report set volume or achieved RIR. This single-exercise result does not establish universal equivalence or negate the different single-set trial. [vasconcelos-2026-leg-extension-rir]

Measurement: People imperfectly predict remaining repetitions, even with training experience. The review found average underprediction of roughly one rep with substantial variation; do not automatically correct every log by one. [halperin-2022-rir-accuracy]

Practice inference: For general hypertrophy, 1–3 RIR is a starting heuristic, adjusted to exercise and recovery, not an exact research prescription. Define the endpoint: zero additional clean reps differs from attempting and failing a rep. Reserve failure practice for an appropriate setup; technique loss is a reason to stop. Scope is general adult resistance training, not rehabilitation or a universal program rule.
