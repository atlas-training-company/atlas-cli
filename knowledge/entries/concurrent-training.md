# Combining resistance and aerobic training

Strength and muscle outcomes: A review of 43 studies in healthy adults found no clear average reduction in whole-muscle hypertrophy or maximal strength from adding aerobic training to an otherwise matched lifting program. Explosive-strength gains were more vulnerable, especially within the same session versus sessions separated by at least three hours. This subgroup finding is not a universal recovery interval or a guarantee at any training dose. [schumann-2022-concurrent]

Aerobic outcomes: A 2026 review found no clear overall difference in VO2max gains between combined training and endurance training alone. Reduced endurance load in the combined programs was associated with smaller gains in untrained-participant and 16-week subgroups. This does not establish a 16-week threshold or show that lifting itself caused the difference. Only the abstract was reviewed; participant totals, detailed protocols and overlap with other reviews were not verified. [zhao-2026-concurrent-aerobic]

Practice inference: When possible, separate demanding conditioning from important lower-body or power sessions. If combining them, prioritize the most important task and monitor its performance. Distinguish adding lifting from replacing endurance work: a time-limited hybrid plan may change both doses. If aerobic progress is the priority, check what endurance work would be displaced before assuming equivalent gains. Adjust total stress when repeated session quality deteriorates. These reviews establish neither a fixed interval quota nor a universally superior aerobic device.
