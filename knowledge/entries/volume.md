# Weekly volume for hypertrophy

Evidence: A meta-regression across 67 studies associated higher weekly set volume with greater muscle growth, with diminishing returns. Its sample was predominantly young and male; the model cannot identify an individual's optimal set count. [pelland-2026-dose-response]

Evidence: A large network meta-analysis found that many prescriptions produced hypertrophy, with multiple-set prescriptions ranking highest. Rankings do not make one combination mandatory. [currier-2023-prescription]

Practice inference: Count challenging working sets separately from warm-ups, record the target muscle, and keep the counting method consistent. Indirect work from compound movements contributes, but a fractional count is an accounting convention, not an exact physiological conversion. Start from a tolerable baseline; consider a small increase for a priority muscle only after checking effort, consistency, and recovery. Repeated performance deterioration is a reason to reassess stress before adding sets. No universal minimum, maximum, or mandatory weekly quota is established here.
