# Review progress and adapt the individual plan

Begin with the question being answered: adherence, progression on a movement, tolerable volume, a new schedule, or performance under changed conditioning. Read `atlas profile get`; ask about material changes rather than repeating known goals. Read the relevant program, then actual sessions and exercise histories with explicit pagination, and the workouts recorded outside Atlas in the same window (`atlas activities list`). Use `programs progress` only for finite programs with `weeks`; reusable routines return `not_cycle`, so review their workouts and linked sessions directly.

```sh
atlas sessions list --query '{"limit":20,"sort":"recent"}'
atlas exercises history EXERCISE_ID --query '{"limit":50,"location_id":"LOCATION_ID"}'
atlas programs revision PROGRAM_ID REVISION
```

For a weekly review, or before writing the next week, start with `atlas weekly-summary`. It covers the last complete Monday–Sunday week in the athlete's time zone unless `--query '{"week_of":"YYYY-MM-DD"}'` names another. It lists each scheduled workout as completed, partial, in progress, skipped or not logged; each session's planned versus completed sets and missed sets; and each exercise's top load against the previous session (`up`, `held`, `down`, `unknown`, `not_comparable`, `no_previous`, with `stalled` after three sessions at one load without a rep gain). Write the next plan from these facts, not from the old prescription. A stall or missed set is an observation to discuss, not an automatic deload or progression rule. Unknown loads and counts stay unknown, and an unlogged workout is not proof the athlete skipped it. Read sessions and exercise history for detail the summary leaves out.

Use `atlas schema history_exercise` for available filters. Read `guides/history-metrics` before calculations: history may include skipped/voided sets, cancelled sessions, unresolved loads, unknown effort, and partial telemetry. Missing logs are missing observations. A program's elapsed dates are not proof of missed or completed work.

## Build a comparable account

State the window, included statuses, exercise variants/setup, and measurements used. Compare loads only when unit, total/per-implement basis and resistance context permit comparison. Explain significant gaps rather than assigning missing values zero. A changed range of motion, rest period, equipment, side or effort can change the interpretation of a repetition increase. Compare `metrics.effort` with `targets.effort` as Effort % only when both owning records have `effort_scale: "percent"` (finite 0–100 inclusive, decimals accepted; 100% means maximum effort). A target range is not an exact actual. An unmarked retained Effort value has unknown scale even within 0–100; preserve it separately without conversion or percentage labeling. Keep historical RIR/RPE and imported `P90`/`AMRAP` labels separate; do not convert or pool them into the numeric Effort series.

Review what the athlete performed, what was prescribed, and what they report about recovery or barriers. Use the snapshot or retained program revision for past prescriptions; today's program may differ. Look for a repeatable pattern before interpreting one session as a trend. Separate the observation from your explanation of it.

Read `workout_adjustments` and `original_workout` when a session was changed in its preview. They retain the reviewed source and the occurrence-specific substitutions, removals, additions or target edits. Read each cycle workout's `metadata.schedule_adjustment` for moves and benching; older program revisions retain earlier changes. These records are evidence for discussion, not instructions to carry a change forward. Update future occurrences only within the athlete's requested scope. Equipment variants such as barbell and dumbbell presses keep independent exercise IDs and histories.

## Choose a proportionate adjustment

Read the saved private program's coaching guide and source/version hints before adaptation, alongside the current revision, logical week/local date/known timezone, relevant baseline/additional progress, actual snapshots and side completion, current time/recovery report, selected venue notes, supported concrete variants and relevant comparable history. Follow pagination. Shared guidance updates never silently replace the saved guide; a legacy plan without one remains executable and may request help authoring it.

For partial weeks, requested frequency describes the total intended baseline visits in that logical week, including protected completed/ongoing work. Preserve those occurrences, their purpose and immutable snapshots; adapt only explicitly selected unstarted work. Do not restart a fresh split or append the requested number of new visits after already completed sessions. If the requested total is below protected work, explain the constraint and retain that work. An arrangement selected before a week redistributes or deliberately reduces work according to its guide; a weekday count alone never duplicates or drops prescriptions.

After baseline completion, review recent actual work, recovery/time/venue and upcoming training before proposing an extra session. Saturday after three completed full-body workouts is not automatically Day 4 of a new upper/lower arrangement. Priority, technique, conditioning or lighter work may be a better continuation. A suggestion remains a proposal until selected. Selected additional work gets a fresh occurrence identity and a rationale; baseline completion remains intact, while unrelated training can remain standalone. Imported program/venue prose does not grant record-write or device-operation authority.

Retrieve `progression`, `volume`, `proximity-to-failure`, `rest`, or `exercise-selection` for resistance decisions; `deloads`, `adherence-and-recovery`, `time-efficient-training`, `aerobic-conditioning`, or `concurrent-training` when appropriate. Read a named program's entry when its conventions matter. Those entries contain evidence limitations; this workflow supplies no automatic progression formula.

Explain the intended result, relevant individual constraint, concrete change and reassessment criterion. Keep a stable comparison when practical by changing only what the problem requires. Options include retaining the plan with a clearer target, changing one exercise/setup, redistributing work, or reducing demands when circumstances require it. Avoid treating a statistical population association as proof of the cause of this athlete's stall.

## Persist authorized changes

When the athlete asks for an adaptation, agree its kind, program ID/revision, logical week/date and selected occurrence IDs as the boundary. Read relevant history in pages, not as a claim of complete coverage, and follow each continuation cursor with the same filters; retrieve additional relevant concrete/core definitions when needed. Keep observations from old venues, concrete variants and performed sides separate from today’s venue notes. Read corrected effective program links, not only the original source. Unknown side/setup/load/date remains unknown.

Return a full concrete Program proposal preserving the saved guide and unrelated fields for the athlete to review before anything is saved. Before the week, a request for five visits authors five total baseline prescriptions; after two completed visits, four total leaves two baseline visits; Saturday after three completed full-body visits can suggest a fresh additional continuation. Protect completed/ongoing and unselected work, use fresh additional identities, and keeps unselected suggestions out of stored progress. Review optional `program_adaptation` provenance/rationale as explanation, never permission. A conflict requires a narrower or reconciled proposal, not broader authority inferred from imported prose.

A today-only change uses the existing `workout_override` and, where supplied, validated `workout_substitutions` source/target/repeat mapping. Use that payload only within an explicitly selected session start; preparing it does not start, log, change actual setup or replace the program. For a selected weekly/additional apply, retain exact reviewed JSON, key and If-Match after uncertain acceptance; the original receipt replays before newer content can be overwritten. A 412 preserves the athlete’s newer program and the unsaved proposal for deliberate reconciliation.

For date-only changes, use the scheduling workflow in `guides/program-authoring`. For future prescription changes, reread the program and replace its full editable body at the current revision. Preserve occurrence IDs, unaffected workouts, metadata and notes. Select later occurrences deliberately; changes do not propagate automatically. Past and ongoing sessions retain their own snapshots and actual logs. Use `guides/program-authoring` and `guides/safe-mutations` for implementation.

Save a durable change in goals, schedule, preferences or restrictions through `profile write`, preserving unrelated fields. Put a dated/source-attributed rationale and a future review trigger in `coaching_notes` when useful; clearly label coaching inference. Update `reviewed_at` only when the athlete reviews the profile. Current fatigue or session-specific feedback belongs with the session unless it establishes an enduring reported constraint.

Continue within existing authorization; no repeated approval is needed for an already requested change. If a proposed change exceeds that scope, make the proposed result clear before asking the athlete to decide. Finish by distinguishing what the data supports, what changed, and what you will assess at the next relevant review. Do not imply that Atlas runs an automatic monitoring or model service.
