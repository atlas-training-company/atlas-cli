# Exercise selection, equipment and range of motion

Evidence: Direct comparisons found no clear hypertrophy advantage for free weights over machines. Strength outcomes depended on the trained and tested task, so practice the relevant movement when a specific strength test matters. [haugen-2023-equipment]

Practice inference: For muscle growth, choose an exercise that loads the intended muscle, allows consistent execution and progression, and fits the person's tolerance and equipment. Retain useful movements long enough to compare performance; substitute when fit or availability prevents productive training.

Applicability and limits: The review included healthy adults aged 18–60 and only five hypertrophy studies. It excluded several specialized devices and free-motion cables, so it does not directly validate every cable or motorized resistance system. Comparable average growth also does not imply identical biomechanics, individual comfort, or injury risk. Equipment choice is a practical inference rather than a universal ranking.

Range of motion (ROM): A review of eight studies found a small average hypertrophy advantage for partial repetitions at longer versus shorter muscle lengths. This comparison does not establish superiority over full-ROM training. Quadriceps studies predominated; only the abstract and public reference metadata were reviewed, so detailed protocols and trial overlap remain unverified. [strey-2026-partial-rom]

An eight-week trial in trained adults found similar measured elbow-flexor and elbow-extensor growth with deliberately prescribed lengthened partials and full ROM. Thirty participants enrolled; each participant’s upper limbs were randomized between the two conditions, and 25 participants completed testing. The small sample, measured muscle sites and ROM-specific failure endpoints limit generalization; similar findings do not prove universal equivalence. [wolf-2025-lengthened-partials]

Practice inference: Use a comfortable, repeatable ROM that fits the intended muscle and goal. Deliberate lengthened partials are an option, not a requirement to replace productive full-ROM training or chase extreme stretch. Record a planned ROM change and establish a new comparison baseline; unexpectedly shortening repetitions as load rises is not comparable progress. Retrieve `progression` for the existing comparison and adjustment procedure.

Fatigue cost (practice inference): Exercises that grow a muscle similarly can cost the athlete very differently. Each catalog exercise carries a `fatigue_rating` and a `limiting_factor`; retrieve `guides/stimulus-fatigue` to choose the lowest-fatigue version that still loads the target muscle and to keep a session's stacked fatigue under its caps.
