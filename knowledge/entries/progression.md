# Progression and load adjustments

Practice framework: Compare repetitions and load under similar technique, range of motion, rest, and effort. More load with substantially shorter repetitions is not directly comparable progress. The supplied book notes emphasize repeatable execution and gradual overload. [nippard-2024-muscle-ladder]

Coaching heuristic: Double progression means adding repetitions within an agreed range before increasing load. When the planned working sets reach the upper target with the intended effort and consistent execution, consider the smallest useful load increase. If available increments are too large, keep the load and improve repeatability. This is an implementation choice, not an experimentally established optimal algorithm.

Evidence: Two short trials in untrained adults compared progressive loading with an unchanged prescription:

- In 31 young adults, opposite legs performed knee extensions for 12 weeks, three sets of 10–12 repetitions twice weekly. Both conditions increased muscle cross-sectional area, with no statistically clear difference at individual assessment times. Growth specifically between sessions 18 and 24 favored progression (difference in change 1.22 cm², 95% CI 0.59–1.84). Progression also maintained higher effort and accumulated more volume load, so this does not isolate load increases from those differences. [ferrari-2026-progressive-overload]
- In 41 young women, 82 arms were allocated to progressive loading, fixed load/repetitions or nonexercise control. Eight weeks of elbow flexion, three sets of 8–12 repetitions three times weekly, increased muscle thickness in both training conditions, with greater gains under progression. The abstract gives no effect estimate or uncertainty interval. [kassiano-2026-progressive-overload]

Interpretation: Reassess challenge as the athlete adapts; unchanged loading can still produce initial growth. These distinct trials do not compare progression algorithms or establish a universal increment or timetable. Only their abstracts and publication records were reviewed; trained athletes, other exercises and longer-term outcomes remain uncertain. A session without a load increase is not automatically unsuccessful.

Limitation: RIR is a subjective estimate with meaningful error. Use repeated sessions and context rather than one unusually easy or hard set to justify a lasting change. [halperin-2022-rir-accuracy]

Applicability: General adult training with stable exercises. A named program's explicit progression policy takes precedence until an authorized revision changes it; this guidance supplies neither universal load increments nor a reason to force progress through pain.
