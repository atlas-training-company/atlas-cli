# Assessment and readiness

Guidance: CSEP's Get Active Questionnaire is intended to help most people become active while identifying circumstances needing individual advice. It considers current symptoms, relevant conditions, existing activity restrictions, and recent activity. A positive response leads to its companion guidance, not an automatic prohibition on all exercise. These summaries do not administer or replace the questionnaire. [csep-2017-get-active]

Coaching procedure: Establish the person's priority, training experience, current activity, available days and minutes, equipment, preferences, and relevant restrictions. Read existing answers first and ask only for missing or changed information. Distinguish what the athlete reports from what logs show; absent logs do not establish inactivity, and an unreported restriction is not a confirmed absence of restrictions.

Coaching procedure: Choose a repeatable starting assessment that serves the goal, such as technically consistent submaximal repetitions or a tolerable walk/cycle duration. Record the exercise, setup, load, repetitions, effort, symptoms, and conditions needed for comparison. A maximal lift or exhaustive conditioning test is not required simply to create a baseline. The exact assessment is a coaching choice, not a universal validated battery.

Immediate escalation: Address current concerning symptoms before any intake, record retrieval, or logging. Stop exercise for new chest pressure/discomfort with light-headedness, shortness of breath, or other possible heart-attack warning signs and advise calling local emergency services promptly (911 in Canada/US). Symptoms easing or recurring does not establish safety. Use emergency medical services for transport rather than driving yourself; follow the dispatcher's instructions. Do not diagnose the cause, suggest switching exercises, or delay help to collect training data. [aha-2024-heart-attack-warning-signs] [aha-2015-first-aid-chest-pain]

Readiness boundary: Recheck relevant changes before increasing training demands. Follow known clinician restrictions. Exercise-related dizziness, fainting, breathlessness at rest, or new pain/swelling affecting activity warrant appropriate health-professional advice; urgency depends on the symptoms, with possible emergency signs handled immediately as above. For an existing condition, use the person's established guidance rather than assume exercise is forbidden. [csep-2017-get-active-reference]

Applicability: General adult coaching and information gathering. This entry does not diagnose symptoms, provide rehabilitation, certify medical clearance, or require every athlete to obtain a new clearance before ordinary training. If illness or an unresolved symptom changes the proposed session, narrow or defer that session and explain the reason.
