# Integrate conditioning and recovery

Read guidance by ID with the Atlas `knowledge` tool (`get` action) or `atlas knowledge get ID`. Start with `guides/coaching-context` and `guides/hybrid-decisions`; `aerobic-conditioning` and `concurrent-training` hold the evidence.

From Atlas and the athlete, confirm the current priority, lifting schedule, session time, conditioning history, available equipment and reported readiness.

1. Identify the purpose: aerobic base, VO2-focused work, sport performance, enjoyment or temporary lower-stress activity. Match the proposal to that goal and current capacity.
2. Place conditioning around priority lifting and the actual calendar. Consider overlapping muscular demand, technical quality and the following session, not only nominal duration.
3. Choose a familiar, tolerable modality and an interpretable starting dose. Separate warm-up, hard work, recovery and cool-down. A population dose model does not determine exact intervals.
4. Define progression or reduction from observed performance and tolerability. Review total demand before assuming cardio is the cause of stalled lifting. Do not infer fatigue from incomplete logs.
5. For recovery concerns, separate ordinary training adjustment from symptoms that need professional assessment (`assessment-and-readiness`). For a movement practice, use `skills/training-mobility-yoga`.

Deliver placement, dose rationale, assumptions, what to track and a reassessment condition. Give a conditional structure when missing context prevents exact dosing. Save conditioning work to the Atlas program only when the athlete asked or approves.
